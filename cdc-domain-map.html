Two papers, one route

Cycle Double Cover Domain Map

The 2026 proof of the cycle double cover conjecture never attacks cycles directly. It carries the question out of graphs, through flows and edge labels, into linear algebra over 𝔽2, and settles it there with a parity count. This page maps every domain the two papers pass through and the mapping that connects each pair.

1 Bridgeless graph → Cubic 3-edge-connected graph Take a counterexample with fewest edges. A 2-edge-cut is removed by contracting one of its edges and lifting the smaller cover back. A vertex of degree 4 or more is split with Fleischner's splitting lemma, which detaches two of its edges into one new edge while keeping the graph 2-edge-connected. Every vertex now meets exactly three edges, so every later local identity has three terms and the flow value on one edge is the sum of the other two. Oum Prop. 4, Lemma 3
OpenAI cites Jaeger [3]
2 Cubic 3-edge-connected graph → Three spanning trees, no common edge Add a parallel copy of every edge; the result is 6-edge-connected. Kundu's corollary of the Tutte and Nash-Williams tree-packing theorem gives three edge-disjoint spanning trees there. Projected back, no edge lies in all three. Connectivity, a statement about all cuts, becomes three concrete rigid objects whose complements generate the cycle space. Oum Thm 5, Cor. 6, Lemma 7
3 Three spanning trees → Nowhere-zero 𝔽23-flow For each tree Ti, take the symmetric difference of the fundamental cycles of all edges outside Ti. The result Fi has even degree everywhere and contains every non-tree edge. The three indicator vectors form φ. OpenAI reaches the same object by citing the 8-flow theorem and Tutte's group-flow theorem instead. A packing fact becomes an algebraic object that satisfies a linear conservation law at every vertex, and nonzero-ness is guaranteed edge by edge. Oum Lemma 8, Lemma 10
OpenAI §2 (Kilpatrick, Jaeger, Tutte)
4 Flow → Linear system over 𝔽2 At a vertex the three flow values are nonzero, distinct, and sum to zero, so the sets {t + φ(f), t + φ(g)} already use every value 0 or 2 times locally. The only obstacle is that the two ends of an edge must build the same set. That is the condition tu + tv ∈ de + ⟨φ(e)⟩ (Oum), or tu + tv + εe f(e) = de with an extra bit per edge (OpenAI). "Does a global labelling exist?" becomes "is this linear system solvable?", with unknowns indexed by vertices and equations by edges. OpenAI eq. (2)–(4)
Oum Lemma 12, eq. (1)
5 Linear system → Orthogonality check Oum: build the block matrix A and use column space = (left nullspace)⊥, so A t = b is solvable iff b is orthogonal to every y with Aᵀy = 0. OpenAI: d ∈ im L iff every dual family η that vanishes on im L also kills d. Either way the certificate is one vector he per edge with he · φ(e) = 0 and zero sum at each vertex. Finding a solution is replaced by showing every would-be certificate of infeasibility vanishes, and certificates are local: a vertex condition and an edge condition. OpenAI eq. (5)–(6)
Oum Lemma 13, Prop. 14, eq. (2)
6 Orthogonality check → Parity at one vertex Fix a cubic vertex. The six pairings of the three he with the three φ(e) collapse to a single bit λ, and λ equals the number of nonzero he at that vertex, mod 2. The vertex's contribution to Σ he · de is exactly λ. A computation in a 3-dimensional vector space becomes a count of nonzero edges at a vertex. OpenAI eq. (7)–(9)
Oum eq. (3)
7 Parity → The tv exist Summing the vertex contributions counts each edge with he ≠ 0 twice, once at each end. Twice anything is 0 in 𝔽2, so every certificate vanishes and the system is solvable. Double counting closes the argument. The fact that an edge has two ends is precisely what characteristic 2 erases. OpenAI Lemma 2.2
Oum Prop. 15
8 Flow + tv → Two-element edge labels Set Pe = tv + φ(f) + ⟨φ(e)⟩ for either endpoint v and either other edge f at v. Step 7 makes it independent of the endpoint; φ(e) ≠ 0 makes it a genuine pair. At every vertex each value of 𝔽23 appears in 0 or 2 of the three sets. A proper 3-edge-colouring is the special case {0, e₁}, {0, e₂}, {e₁, e₂}. A relaxed 3-edge-colouring: exactly the local evenness that a cover needs, without asking the graph to be 3-edge-colourable. OpenAI eq. (3), Lemma 2.1 remark
Oum Lemma 12
9 Two-element edge labels → Cycle double cover For each value s, the edge set Hs = {e : s ∈ Pe} has even degree at every vertex, so it is Eulerian and splits into cycles. Each edge lies in exactly |Pe| = 2 of the eight layers. The cover is read off layer by layer. Eight values give an 8-cycle double cover, which is also the bound Oum records as Theorem 18. Oum Lemma 11, Thm 18

Where the two writeups take different roads

The route is the same in both papers. Four legs are travelled with different vehicles, and each difference is a choice of domain: Oum swaps a cited theorem for a self-contained construction, and an abstract object for a concrete one.

OpenAI §2

Oum §2–3

Proves the reduction from scratch with Fleischner's splitting lemma and 2-edge-cut contraction.

OpenAI

Nowhere-zero 8-flow theorem (Kilpatrick, Jaeger), converted to an 𝔽23-flow by Tutte's group-flow theorem. Domain: integer flows ⇔ group flows.

Oum §4

Tree packing (Tutte, Nash-Williams, Kundu) and fundamental cycles. Never mentions integer 8-flows. Domain: spanning trees ⇒ cycle space.

Neighbouring domains the papers point to

Oum's closing section and the OpenAI introduction mention several other translations of the same problem. None is needed for the proof, but each is another domain the cycle double cover question can be moved into, sometimes gaining strength and sometimes losing it.

  • Surfaces → cycles. A strong embedding has every face boundary a cycle, and the face boundaries of a 2-cell embedding cover every edge twice. The strong embedding conjecture would imply the theorem; the theorem now settles it for cubic graphs. Planar graphs were the first known case, by facial cycles of blocks. Oum Conj. 2; OpenAI §1.
  • Colourings ⇔ covers. For loopless cubic graphs, 3-edge-colourable, having a 3-cycle double cover, and having a 4-cycle double cover are equivalent. The Petersen graph shows 4 layers are not always enough; the proof gives 8; the open conjecture asks for 5. Oum Thm 19, Conj. 20; OpenAI §1 (Szekeres).
  • Covers → flows. An orientable k-cycle double cover yields a nowhere-zero ℤk-flow, so the orientable 5-cover conjecture would imply Tutte's 5-flow conjecture. Oum Lemma 22, Conj. 21.
  • Flows → covers. The seven nonzero linear combinations of the three coordinate subgraphs of an 𝔽23-flow form a 7-cycle 4-cover; Seymour's 6-flow gives a 10-cycle 6-cover. Oum Thm 23 (Bermond, Jackson, Jaeger), Thm 24 (Fan).
  • Matchings ⇔ covers. Complementing each of six perfect matchings that cover every edge twice gives a 6-cycle 4-cover, so the Berge–Fulkerson conjecture is a cover statement in disguise. Oum Conj. 25, 26.
  • Graphs → binary matroids. The conjecture is equivalent to the same statement for binary matroids without coloops and without an F7* minor, so the theorem covers all regular matroids. The bounded-size versions do not transfer: the cographic matroid of Kn needs n cycles. Oum Thm 27 (Jamshy, Tarsi), §9.5.
  • Integer flows ⇔ group flows. Tutte's group-flow theorem lets OpenAI trade a nowhere-zero 8-flow for an 𝔽23-flow, the domain where characteristic 2 makes orientation irrelevant. OpenAI §2 (Tutte [9]).

What the pattern teaches

Every hop trades an existence question for a more rigid object. Bridgelessness becomes cubic vertices, cubic vertices become three trees, trees become a flow, a flow becomes coset conditions, cosets become a linear system, and the system becomes a duality check. None of these hops is deep on its own.

The payoff is the last step. Once the question is "does this vector lie in a column space?", it can be attacked by showing every certificate against it vanishes, and the certificate sum turns out to count each edge at both of its ends. The cover is a double cover, the field has characteristic 2, and a graph edge has exactly two ends. The proof works because those three twos are the same two.
